\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report records a remediation pass over the Collagen--Gallic-Acid
crosslinking repository, carried out under three standing constraints:
\textbf{do not alter any laboratory or docking result}, \textbf{do not
substitute synthetic values that have no reference}, and \textbf{report
everything}. Twelve defects were repaired in code and pinned by tests. One new
finding emerged that no code change can repair: for three of the nine ligands,
including the headline compound, the molecule that was docked is not the
molecule it is named after. The docking scores were left exactly as released.
\end{abstract}

% =====================================================================
\section{Ground rules observed}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{No laboratory or docking value was modified.} No
        $\Delta G$, no Vinardo score, no ITC measurement, no yield. Where a
        recorded number is wrong, that is reported --- not overwritten.
  \item \textbf{No synthetic data without a reference.} Every corrected
        chemical value traces to PubChem, IUPAC nomenclature, or a cited
        paper. The one quantity that was \emph{derived} rather than looked up
        --- the missing MMP-1 box centres --- is derived from the released
        docked poses, written to a separate file marked
        \code{is\_measured=false}, validated against known ground truth, and
        deliberately \emph{not} wired into training.
  \item \textbf{Corrections that change results are declared as such.} The
        feature fixes below alter every feature vector. A
        \code{FEATURE\_SCHEMA\_VERSION} is now stamped into each results file
        so no number can be silently attributed to the wrong code.
\end{itemize}

% =====================================================================
\section{Part 1 --- Programming and model}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Metrics: the headline statistic was computed with the wrong estimator}

Spearman's $\rho$ is this study's headline metric. It was computed with

\[
  \rho = 1 - \frac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)}
\]

which is valid \emph{only when no values are tied}, and ranked with
\code{argsort(argsort(\,))}, which breaks ties by row position. Vinardo writes
affinities to two decimal places, so ties are guaranteed --- and the reported
$\rho$ therefore depended on the row order of the CSV.

Replaced with Pearson's $r$ on tie-averaged ranks, the textbook definition.
Verified to match \code{scipy.stats.spearmanr} to $10^{-10}$ and to be exactly
invariant under row permutation, which the previous estimator was not (it
drifted by $8\times10^{-4}$ on a representative permutation).

\warn{Honest note:} on this data the tie bias is small. It does \emph{not}
explain the gap between the reported $\rho = 0.93$ and the measured $0.163$.
That gap remains open (P4.1).

\subsection{$R^2$ is now reported}

No coefficient of determination appeared anywhere. Correlation cannot separate
a useful model from a useless one; only
$R^2 = 1 - \mathrm{SS_{res}}/\mathrm{SS_{tot}}$ answers ``does this beat
predicting the mean?'', and it goes negative when the answer is no. It is now
computed per fold and aggregated across folds. Note this is the
regression-against-the-mean form, not $r^2$: the two differ exactly when
predictions are biased or mis-scaled, which is the failure mode worth
catching.

\subsection{Results are now traceable to the code that produced them}

Each results file carries a \code{provenance} block: git commit and dirty
flag, Python and torch versions, resolved device, feature-schema version, and
the ligand-identity verdict. Two of the defects found in this audit --- a
feature that was identically zero, and a shipped cache built by an older
version --- were invisible precisely because nothing recorded which code wrote
which number.

\subsection{Mixed target quantities are now impossible to use silently}

\code{--include-transfer} pools anchor rows (Vinardo docking \emph{scores})
with PDBbind rows (\emph{experimental} binding affinities). Both are written
to \code{y} in kcal/mol and averaged into one RMSE and one $\rho$. Nothing
warned. The released record count, $6{,}196 + 5{,}000 = 11{,}196$, indicates
the published metrics were computed on such a mixture. A prominent warning now
fires and the composition is recorded on the dataset object. The default was
left unchanged so that no existing command silently changes meaning.

\subsection{Environment pinned}

\code{requirements.txt} gives supported ranges; the new
\code{requirements-lock.txt} gives the one combination known to run end to end
(Python 3.14.3, torch 2.13.0, RDKit 2026.3.5, NumPy 2.5.2 on Darwin arm64), so
a reviewer can reproduce a number rather than approximately reproduce it.

\section{Part 2 --- False biochemical data}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Fixed: the galloyl detector matched nothing}

\code{\_SMARTS\_GALLOYL = "Oc1cc(O)c(O)cc1"} encodes 1,3,4-trihydroxybenzene.
The hydroxyls are not three-adjacent, so it matched \bad{no ligand in the
catalogue} --- \code{galloyl\_strict} was identically zero for all nine,
gallic acid included, and its weight never contributed. The score was carried
instead by the catechol and pyrogallol patterns, which \emph{both} match every
trihydroxyphenyl ring, giving $1.00 + 0.67 = 1.67$ per ring.

The counting scheme was also wrong in a second way: matches were counted per
SMARTS hit and subtracted (\code{ca - g - py}), but a benzene-1,2,3-triol
yields \emph{two} catechol hits, so one spurious catechol survived on every
trihydroxy ring. Counting is now done at ring level with the three nested
classes made disjoint.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Ligand} & \textbf{Before} & \textbf{After} & \textbf{Reference} \\
\midrule
gallic acid          & 1.67 & \ok{1.00} & one galloyl unit \\
pyrogallol           & 1.67 & \ok{1.00} & one pyrogallol ring, no acyl \\
protocatechuic acid  & 0.67 & \ok{0.67} & one catechol \\
ellagic acid         & 1.34 & \ok{1.34} & two catechol rings \\
pentagalloylglucose  & 8.35 & \ok{5.00} & five galloyl units \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Fixed: phenolic hydroxyls were over-counted}

\code{total\_aromatic\_oh} accepted any oxygen of degree 1--2 bonded to an
aromatic carbon, with no hydrogen requirement. RDKit perceives ellagic acid's
fused lactone rings as aromatic, so its two ester oxygens and two lactone
carbonyls were scored as phenolic hydroxyls: \bad{8 against 4 actual}. All
nine ligands now match reference values.

\subsection{Fixed: the pH encoding was thermodynamically impossible}

The condition encoder read a hardcoded table
$f = \{5.0{:}\,0.85,\ 5.5{:}\,0.15,\ 7.0{:}\,0.02\}$. No single p$K_a$
produces it. Fitting $f = 0.85$ at pH 5.0 implies $\mathrm{p}K_a = 5.75$,
which predicts $0.64$ at pH 5.5 and $0.05$ at pH 7.0. More fundamentally,
Henderson--Hasselbalch bounds any single titratable site to a drop of at most
$0.76 \to 0.24$ over half a pH unit, and only when centred exactly on its
p$K_a$; the table claimed $0.85 \to 0.15$.

It was also a \emph{second} pH model contradicting the correct
Henderson--Hasselbalch implementation already present in
\code{graph/residue\_data.py} --- and the wrong one was the one reaching the
network. The fractions are now derived from a single p$K_a$,

\[
  f(\mathrm{pH}) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{\,\mathrm{pH} - \mathrm{p}K_a}},
  \qquad \mathrm{p}K_a(\mathrm{GLU}) = 4.07,
\]

after Olsson \emph{et al.}, \emph{J.\ Chem.\ Theory Comput.} 7(2):525--537
(2011) --- the reference already used elsewhere in the codebase --- giving
$\{5.0{:}\,0.1051,\ 5.5{:}\,0.0358,\ 7.0{:}\,0.0012\}$. A test asserts the
table equals the formula, so a hand-typed value cannot return, and a second
test asserts the two modules agree for GLU at every pH.

\warn{Recorded caveat.} The corrected fractions span 0.001--0.105, about
$8\times$ narrower than the old, incorrect 0.02--0.85. The encoding is now
right but poorly conditioned as a network input beside features on $[0,1]$.
The principled reparameterisation is the HH exponent
$(\mathrm{p}K_a - \mathrm{pH})$, which is linear in pH and well scaled --- but
that is a \emph{modelling} change rather than a correctness fix, so it was
documented and not applied.

\subsection{Fixed: the NHS ester intermediate was internally inconsistent}

The entry declared MW 285.22 and 10 heavy atoms, matching neither each other
nor its own SMILES. Rather than guess, the shipped docking inputs were read.
The EDC/NHS series is built on \emph{propionic acid} as the carboxylic-acid
stand-in --- catalogue and structure file agree on the O-propanoylisourea ---
so the consistent NHS-stage species is N-succinimidyl propionate,
$\mathrm{C_7H_9NO_4}$, MW 171.15, 12 heavy atoms. The catalogue is now
\ok{9 of 9 self-consistent} between SMILES, formula, MW and heavy-atom count.

\section{Part 3 --- The finding that code cannot fix}
% =====================================================================

Nothing in the pipeline had ever compared the molecule \emph{named} in the
catalogue against the molecule the docking campaign actually \emph{read from
disk}. Doing so shows that three of nine ligands were docked as a different
compound. The result is identical in the v1 and v2 Drive snapshots, so it is
not a regression from the dataset update.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Ligand} & \textbf{Named} & \textbf{Docked} \\
\midrule
pentagalloylglucose & $\mathrm{C_{41}H_{32}O_{26}}$, 940.68, \textbf{5 galloyl}
  & \bad{$\mathrm{C_{27}H_{24}O_{18}}$, 636.47, \textbf{3 galloyl}} \\
NHS & $\mathrm{C_4H_5NO_3}$, 115.09
  & \bad{$\mathrm{C_3H_3NO_3}$, 101.06} \\
NHS ester intermediate & $\mathrm{C_7H_9NO_4}$, 171.15, 5-ring
  & \bad{$\mathrm{C_6H_7NO_4}$, 157.12, 4-ring} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Pentagalloylglucose.} The input \code{.sdf} contains four
disconnected fragments: a tri-\emph{O}-galloylglucose plus three loose gallic
acid molecules. Docking retained only the largest. All 68 released PGG poses
are tri-\emph{O}-galloylglucose --- three galloyl units, not five.

\paragraph{The two NHS species are one defect.} A succinimide ring has four
ring carbons; both structures were built with three. The deficiency is exactly
$\mathrm{CH_2}$ (14.03\,Da) in each case. One upstream structure-building
error propagated to every NHS-containing species.

\paragraph{Why this matters most.} A binding affinity is meaningful only
beside the structure it was computed for. Every $\Delta G$ labelled
\emph{pentagalloylglucose} belongs to a molecule carrying three of the five
galloyl groups whose count is the paper's proposed mechanism --- and PGG is the
headline ligand. The other six ligands (gallic acid, EDC, EDC
\emph{O}-acylisourea, protocatechuic acid, pyrogallol, ellagic acid)
\ok{match exactly} and are unaffected.

\paragraph{What was done, and not done.} The scores were left untouched;
they belong to the molecules that were actually docked and cannot be corrected
by editing a catalogue. The remedy is to re-dock the three structures, which
is a laboratory/compute decision. To prevent recurrence,
\code{Graph\_model/data/provenance.py} now compares every catalogue entry
against its docking input by molecular formula and InChIKey skeleton. It runs
in the training preflight, is recorded in every results file under
\code{provenance.ligand\_identity}, and is asserted by tests --- a mismatch
outside the three known ones fails the suite.

\section{Part 4 --- MMP-1 box centres: derived, disclosed, not adopted}
% =====================================================================

Every MMP-1 record is built with an empty receptor. The root cause is that
\code{mmp1\_collagenase\_docking\_results.csv} has 13 columns against the
collagen file's 44 and carries no \code{box\_center\_*}, so
\code{hetero\_dataset.py} defaults the centre to the origin --- tens of
angstroms from a protein spanning $z \in [13.5, 57.9]$.

The geometry was never recorded, but the poses were, and a pose can only be
generated inside its search box. \code{scripts/reconstruct\_mmp1\_boxes.py}
takes the centroid of all pose heavy atoms per (receptor, box) group.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Validated where the answer is known.} Applying the identical
        procedure to collagen: 31 boxes, \ok{mean error 4.94\,\AA},
        median 4.78, range 1.39--7.94. Against a 20\,\AA{} box this recovers
        the right binding \emph{region}, not the original box.
  \item \textbf{Coverage.} \ok{67 of 67} (receptor, box) pairs recovered from
        335 poses.
  \item \textbf{Occupancy --- the test that matters.} The defect is not the
        missing centre but the empty box. At the origin all three receptors
        yield \bad{0 atoms}. The reconstructed centres yield a median of
        \ok{148--220 receptor atoms}, with \ok{61 of 67} boxes non-empty.
  \item \textbf{Six boxes remain unusable} and are named in the output. Their
        poses sit off the receptor surface, so the centroid is not a box
        centre.
  \item \textbf{Box \emph{size} is not recoverable.} Pose extent is a lower
        bound, not a measurement, and is emitted under a name that says so.
\end{itemize}

A receptor-matching bug in the first draft of this script silently merged
\code{porcine\_MMP1\_966C\_apo} into \code{porcine\_MMP1\_966C} --- two
different structures. It was caught by the coverage count (53/67 instead of
67/67) and fixed by matching receptor names against a known list rather than
by regular expression. Noted here because it is exactly the class of error
this report is about.

Output is a \emph{sidecar} CSV with \code{method} and \code{is\_measured=false}
columns. The shipped CSVs are untouched, and the reconstruction is
\textbf{not} wired into training. Reconstructed geometry is not measured
geometry; whether to adopt it is the authors' call. The authoritative values
should come from the original Vina/Vinardo configuration files, which
additionally resolve which box and which receptor each row refers to.

\section{Part 5 --- v2 LOLO result, and what $R^2$ revealed}
% =====================================================================

The full 9-fold LOLO run on the updated (v2) dataset completed in 3.11\,h on
MPS, seed 0, anchor-only, 200-epoch cap with early stopping.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{RMSE} & \textbf{Pearson $r$} & \textbf{Spearman $\rho$} \\
\midrule
Table~IV claim        & 1.33  & ---   & \textbf{0.93} \\
Measured, v1 data     & 1.047 & 0.291 & \bad{0.163} \\
Measured, v2 data     & \textbf{0.903} & 0.349 & \bad{\textbf{0.190}} \\
\midrule
Baseline: global mean & 1.029 & & \\
Baseline: per-box mean& 0.935 & & \\
Baseline: per-ligand mean (oracle) & \textbf{0.547} & & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The v2 data improves RMSE by 14\% and $\rho$ from 0.163 to 0.190, and 5 of 9
folds now beat their baseline (was 4 of 9). \bad{The gap to the claimed
$\rho = 0.93$ is unchanged in character.}

\paragraph{The $R^2$ column is the finding.} $R^2$ against each held-out
ligand's own mean is \bad{negative in 8 of 9 folds}, mean $-2.00$:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Held-out ligand} & \textbf{RMSE} & \textbf{sd$(y)$} & \textbf{$R^2$} \\
\midrule
EDC                      & 0.722 & 0.282 & \bad{$-5.55$} \\
EDC \emph{O}-acylisourea & 0.631 & 0.455 & \bad{$-0.93$} \\
NHS                      & 0.706 & 0.312 & \bad{$-4.11$} \\
NHS ester intermediate   & 0.459 & 0.378 & \bad{$-0.48$} \\
ellagic acid             & 1.429 & 0.707 & \bad{$-3.08$} \\
gallic acid              & 0.641 & 0.483 & \bad{$-0.76$} \\
pentagalloylglucose      & 2.491 & 1.400 & \bad{$-2.17$} \\
protocatechuic acid      & 0.417 & 0.469 & \ok{$+0.21$} \\
pyrogallol               & 0.632 & 0.435 & \bad{$-1.11$} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Aggregate}       & \textbf{0.903} & \textbf{0.547} & \bad{\textbf{$-2.00$}} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This is precisely the failure that correlation alone conceals, and the reason
$R^2$ was added. The model beats the global baseline only because it places
each ligand at roughly the right overall \emph{level} --- between-ligand
signal, obtainable from molecular size alone. \emph{Within} a held-out ligand,
which is where the study's actual experimental variables live (pH,
temperature, docking box), it explains none of the variance and is on average
$3\times$ worse than simply predicting that ligand's mean.

A model that cannot resolve the pH and temperature dependence of a single
ligand cannot support a claim about how pH and temperature govern
crosslinking.

\paragraph{Two caveats on these numbers.} First, both runs predate the
feature corrections above --- they are feature schema~1, and the galloyl and
pH fixes change every feature vector, so a schema-2 re-run is required before
any of this is quoted as final. Second, the model's test sets total 6{,}276
rows against 6{,}491 in the CSVs: 3.3\% of rows are dropped, now by explicit
row-dropping on missing targets rather than the previous silent
\code{NaN}$\to$0.0 imputation. The baseline figures were computed on the CSV
rows, so the two differ by that 3.3\%.
